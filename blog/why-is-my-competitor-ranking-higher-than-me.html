Why Is My Competitor Ranking Higher Than Me? How to Find Out and Catch Up

Ravi Bairwa6 Oct 20269 min read

Seeing a competitor above you in Google is frustrating, but it is also information. Someone is winning that search for reasons you can usually identify. This guide shows how to compare fairly, what the common reasons are and how to decide what to change first.

Step 1: Check You Are Comparing the Right Thing

Search from a private window and note the exact page that ranks, not only the competitor's homepage. Results vary by location, device and personalisation, so check from the place your customers search. Also check that the competitor is truly competing for the same search with the same intent, since some competitors only look similar.

Step 2: Compare Search Intent

Look at what kind of page the competitor has: a guide, a product page, a local listing, a comparison. If Google favours a type of page you do not have, that may explain the gap. A well-written service page cannot beat a guide in a search where people want information.

Step 3: Compare Content, Honestly

Read their page next to yours. Does it answer the question more fully, more clearly or more quickly? Does it include examples, data, images, comparisons or steps yours lacks? Is it more current? Be as critical of your own page as you would be of theirs. The gap is often depth and usefulness, not word count.

Step 4: Compare Authority and Links

Check how many relevant sites link to the competitor's page and to their site overall, using a link tool or Search Console for your own side. If their page has strong, relevant links and yours has none, that is a likely reason. Our guide to competitor backlink analysis shows how to find the links worth pursuing, and our guide on whether backlinks still matter explains how much they count.

Step 5: Compare the Site as a Whole

Some competitors win because their whole site is stronger: older, more trusted, with many related pages that support each other. A site with deep coverage of a topic often outranks a single good page on a thin site. Consider whether you need a cluster of related pages instead of one.

Step 6: Compare User Experience

Open both pages on a phone. Which loads faster? Which is easier to read and use? Pop-ups, slow scripts and layout jumps can all hurt. See our Core Web Vitals guide for the main speed measures.

Step 7: Compare Titles and Snippets

A competitor may earn more clicks with a clearer title, which can support rankings over time. Compare how both listings look, and see our guide to impressions without clicks.

Step 8: Consider Brand and Reputation

Well-known brands often benefit from people searching for them by name, linking to them and trusting them. You cannot copy that overnight, but you can build it with consistent quality, reviews and mentions. Local competitors often win on reviews and Business Profile strength, as covered in our guide to near me SEO.

Step 9: Check for Technical Issues of Your Own

Make sure your page is indexed, not canonicalised to another URL, and free of errors. A technical fault on your side can look like a competitor's advantage. See why a website isn't showing on Google.

Turn the Comparison Into a Plan

List the differences you found and rank them by how much they probably matter and how easy they are to fix. Improving content and fixing technical issues are usually quicker than building authority, so start there, and run link-building in parallel if the gap in authority is large.

When You Should Not Chase a Competitor

Some searches are dominated by brands you cannot outrank soon. Rather than fight them, target more specific searches where you can win, such as a particular service, place or customer type. A smaller win you can hold is better than a constant loss.

What Not to Do

Do not copy a competitor's page, since a copy gives Google no reason to prefer yours. Do not buy links in bulk to catch up. Do not make dozens of changes at once. And do not judge progress after a few weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions About Competitors Outranking You

Why does my competitor rank above me with worse content? Rankings reflect many factors, so they may have more authority, a better-matched page type, stronger links, a more trusted site or better user experience, even if their writing looks weaker.

How long does it take to outrank a competitor? It depends on the gap and the competition, and closing a large authority gap usually takes months, not weeks.

Should I copy my competitor's page? No. Study what makes it useful and then make something clearly better, since a near-copy gives Google no reason to rank it above the original.

Getting Started

Pick one search where a competitor beats you, open both pages side by side and list three concrete ways theirs is better. Fix the easiest first.
