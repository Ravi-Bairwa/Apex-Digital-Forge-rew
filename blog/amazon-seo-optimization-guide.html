Amazon SEO: How A9/A10 Ranking Actually Differs From Google

Ravi Bairwa27 Sep 20266 min read

Amazon SEO runs on a fundamentally different system than the one described in Google's Search Essentials documentation, since Amazon's own ranking algorithm (commonly called A9, now evolved into A10) optimizes for purchase likelihood rather than general relevance.

Amazon Optimizes for Conversion, Not Just Relevance

Google's algorithm tries to answer a searcher's question as well as possible; Amazon's algorithm tries to predict which listing a searcher will actually buy, which means conversion rate, sales velocity, and purchase history all carry weight that has no real Google equivalent.

A listing that ranks well but converts poorly tends to lose position over time, since Amazon reads low conversion as a signal that the listing isn't actually satisfying the search intent, regardless of how well-optimized the text itself is.

The Backend Search Terms Field Still Matters

Unlike a Google meta keywords tag, which Google has ignored for years, Amazon's hidden backend search terms field is actively used in ranking — filling it with genuinely relevant terms not already covered in the visible title and bullets is one of the few pure-metadata levers still worth using.

Title Structure Follows Amazon's Own Conventions, Not Google's

Amazon titles favor a front-loaded structure — brand, then product type, then key differentiating attributes (size, color, quantity) — rather than the more natural-language phrasing that performs better in Google's results, since Amazon's own search and category browsing behavior rewards scannable structure over conversational tone.

Reviews and Ratings Function as a Direct Ranking Input

Review count and average rating aren't just trust signals for shoppers on Amazon — they're a measurable input into the ranking algorithm itself, meaning a strong review base can outrank a more keyword-optimized but thinly-reviewed competing listing.

Sales Velocity Creates a Compounding Advantage

A listing that starts selling well tends to rank better, which drives more sales, which further improves ranking — this feedback loop means early momentum (launch promotions, initial review generation) has outsized long-term impact compared to ongoing keyword tweaks alone.

Images and A+ Content Affect Ranking Indirectly

Amazon doesn't directly rank listings based on image quality, but since images heavily influence conversion rate, and conversion rate is a direct ranking input, investing in professional photography and A+ Content brand pages has a real, if indirect, ranking effect.

Off-Amazon Traffic Is an Increasingly Real Ranking Factor

Amazon has publicly acknowledged rewarding listings that bring in traffic from outside the platform (social media, a seller's own website, paid ads), treating external demand generation as a positive signal rather than something ranking is indifferent to.

Frequently Asked Questions About Amazon SEO

Is Amazon SEO the same as Google SEO applied to product listings? No — Amazon's algorithm weighs conversion and sales velocity far more heavily than Google's, so techniques that work for Google content don't transfer directly. Does keyword stuffing still work on Amazon? No — Amazon's system has grown sophisticated enough to penalize unnatural keyword stuffing in titles and bullets, similar to how Google treats it. How much do reviews really affect Amazon ranking? Significantly — review count and rating function as a direct input into the ranking algorithm, not just a trust signal for shoppers browsing the page.

Getting Started

Audit the backend search terms field first, since it's the fastest, lowest-risk fix available and is frequently left mostly empty even on otherwise well-optimized listings.

A Common Mistake in Amazon Listing Optimization

Sellers sometimes focus entirely on the title and bullet points while leaving the backend search terms field mostly empty, assuming visible copy does all the ranking work. Since the backend field is invisible to shoppers but fully indexed by Amazon's algorithm, that gap wastes a genuinely free ranking opportunity.

Reviewing which specific search terms actually drive impressions in Seller Central's own reporting, rather than guessing at buyer language, tends to surface backend keyword opportunities a listing is currently missing entirely.

Given how directly conversion rate feeds back into ranking, small improvements to product images and bullet clarity often move a listing more than another round of keyword tweaks to text that's already reasonably optimized.

That discipline compounds over time as sales velocity itself starts reinforcing the listing's position.

Sellers who track this correlation over several months tend to make better-informed decisions about where to invest next — better photography, more reviews, or backend keyword refinement — rather than guessing at which lever matters most for their specific category.
