Why Your Website Isn't Showing on Google (and How to Fix It)

Ravi Bairwa6 Oct 20269 min read

If your website does not appear when you search for it, there are two very different possibilities: Google has not indexed your pages, or it has indexed them but ranks them too low to see. The fix depends on which one you have, so the first job is to find out. This guide walks through the checks in order.

First: Is the Site Indexed at All?

Search for your site with the site: operator, such as site:yourdomain.com. If results appear, Google has some pages indexed, and your problem is about ranking or specific pages. If nothing appears, your site is not indexed. Then open Google Search Console, paste a page URL into the URL Inspection bar and read whether the URL is on Google. Use that tool for individual pages, because search operators are only a rough guide.

Cause 1: The Site Is New

A new site can take days or weeks to be found, crawled and indexed. Verify the site in Search Console, submit an XML sitemap and link to the site from somewhere Google already visits. If the site is only a few days old, patience is part of the answer.

Cause 2: A Noindex Tag Is Blocking It

A noindex instruction in the page's meta robots tag, or in an HTTP header, tells Google to keep the page out of results. This is a very common launch mistake: a staging site is blocked from search, then the block is never removed after going live. View the page source and search for "noindex", and check the URL Inspection result.

Cause 3: robots.txt Blocks Crawling

Your robots.txt file can stop Google crawling sections of the site, or all of it. Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt and look for a rule that disallows everything or an important folder. Remember that robots.txt controls crawling and noindex controls indexing, so a blocked page may still appear in some form without useful content.

Cause 4: Google Chose a Different Version of the Page

If you have duplicate or similar pages, wrong canonical tags or both www and non-www versions, Google may index another URL than the one you expected. Check which URL Google reports as canonical in URL Inspection, and make your canonical tags and redirects consistent.

Cause 5: Google Can't Reach the Pages

Server errors, slow responses, broken redirects or pages that load only after complicated scripts can stop pages being crawled or understood. Use the live test in URL Inspection to see what Google receives, and make sure key text and links are in the HTML.

Cause 6: The Pages Are Crawled but Not Indexed

Search Console may show "Discovered" or "Crawled" with "currently not indexed". These statuses mean different things, and our guide to discovered vs crawled, currently not indexed explains what to do for each.

Cause 7: The Content Is Thin or Duplicated

Google does not index everything it finds. Pages that add little, repeat other pages or exist mainly to target keywords may be left out. Make each page genuinely useful, specific and different from the others.

Cause 8: Few or No Links Point to the Site

Google finds many pages through links, and a page nothing links to can be missed or deprioritised. Add internal links from your most visited pages, and earn a few real mentions from relevant sites. Our guide to what a backlink is explains why links matter.

Cause 9: A Manual Action or Security Problem

Open the Manual Actions and Security Issues reports in Search Console. A hacked site or a spam penalty can remove pages from results, and the reports tell you what to fix and how to request a review.

If It Is Indexed but You Can't Find It

If the page is on Google but does not appear for your target searches, you have a ranking problem, not an indexing one. Check that you are searching for terms people really use, and that your page is the best answer to that search. Local businesses should also check their Business Profile, since many searches show the local pack first. See our guide to near me SEO.

A Simple Order of Checks

Check indexing with URL Inspection. Look for noindex and robots.txt blocks. Check canonical and redirect setup. Review Search Console's indexing statuses. Improve thin pages and add internal links. Check for manual actions. Only after that should you start new content or link work.

What Not to Do

Do not submit the same URL over and over, since repeated requests do not speed things up. Do not buy links to "force" indexing. And do not rebuild the site before you know what is wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sites Not Showing on Google

How long does it take for a new website to show on Google? It can take days or weeks, and requesting indexing in Search Console and submitting a sitemap helps Google find it sooner.

Why is my site indexed but not ranking? Indexing only means Google has stored the page. Ranking depends on relevance, content quality, competition and links, so a new or weak page may sit far down the results.

Can a noindex tag stop my site showing? Yes. A noindex tag tells Google to leave the page out, so it is one of the first things to check, especially after a launch.

Getting Started

Run the site: search, inspect your homepage in Search Console and check for noindex and robots.txt blocks. Those three steps find the cause in most cases.
