The Perfect Anchor Text Distribution Strategy That Google Rewards in 2026

Ravi Bairwa1 May 20264 min read

Why Anchor Text Distribution Matters

Anchor text is the clickable text of a hyperlink. Google uses it as a strong signal to understand what a linked page is about. Get it right and rankings improve. Get it wrong and you risk a penalty.

The Four Anchor Text Types

1. Exact Match

The anchor text is the exact keyword you want to rank for. Example: "link building agency India"

Use sparingly — 20–30% maximum. Over-optimizing exact match anchors is one of the most common causes of Google penalties.

2. Partial Match

The anchor includes the keyword but with additional words. Example: "best link building agency for SEO" or "professional link building services"

This is your workhorse anchor type — safe, natural, and effective. Use 25–35%.

3. Branded

The anchor is your brand name. Example: "Apex Digital Forge" or "apexdigitalforge.in"

Branded anchors signal legitimacy and are completely natural. Use 15–20%.

4. Generic / Naked

Anchors like "click here", "read more", "this article", or the raw URL.

These make your link profile look completely natural. Use 15–20%.

The Recommended Distribution

| Anchor Type | Percentage | |---|---| | Exact match | 20–30% | | Partial match | 25–35% | | Branded | 15–20% | | Generic/Naked | 15–20% |

How to Audit Your Current Anchor Text Profile

Before building new links, check what you already have. Pull your existing backlink profile from Ahrefs or Semrush and export the anchor text report. Categorize every anchor into the four types above and calculate your current percentages.

If any single keyword-exact anchor exceeds 30% of your total profile, that is a red flag Google's algorithms are built to catch. This is especially common on sites that built links aggressively in a short window without anchor text planning.

What Triggered This Rule — A Quick History

Google's Penguin update, first rolled out in 2012, specifically targeted sites with unnatural, over-optimized anchor text patterns. Sites that had built hundreds of links all using the exact same keyword anchor saw rankings collapse overnight. Penguin is now part of Google's real-time core algorithm, meaning the anchor text rules described here are not a temporary trend — they are a permanent part of how Google evaluates link profiles.

Anchor Text for Different Page Types

Homepage links tend to receive more branded and naked-URL anchors naturally, since people often link to a homepage by its name or address rather than a specific keyword phrase. Inner pages targeting specific keywords can support a higher share of partial-match anchors without looking unnatural, since the anchor text logically describes what that specific page covers. Matching your anchor strategy to the page type, rather than applying one blanket ratio site-wide, produces a more convincing natural link profile overall.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Never use the same exact anchor on every link
  • Never stuff multiple keywords into one anchor
  • Vary your anchors even within the same type

    • A natural-looking anchor text profile is one of the clearest signals of a legitimate, white-hat link building campaign.
